About

I started my working life in industrial engineering and quality roles, at companies including Hyundai Motor India and Kitex Garments. In 2021 I changed careers. I spent six months, often 16 to 17 hours a day, learning the MERN stack and building projects, including an e-commerce platform and a learning management system.

Since then I have worked on REST APIs, microservices and real-time systems, first as a freelancer and then at Nile Technologies and Ahana Systems. Most of my work is the part users never notice: making services reliable, queries faster and releases uneventful.

Since late 2024 I have also been responsible for people. At Ahana I lead software and application-support teams, plan sprints, review performance, run technical interviews and work through production incidents with the team. I am currently leading three or four projects at once.

Outside work I build small developer tools and publish them on npm, write on the Bodhi Tech blog, teach on YouTube, and run Bodhi Learning, an educational institution in Thrissur, Kerala, which has mentored more than 200 students.

Leadership

  1. Production support is part of the job

    I stay close to live systems. Incident triage and root-cause analysis are work I do with the team, and the goal is a fix that changes the system, not only the symptom.

  2. Standards live in the pipeline

    Code review, SonarQube and CI/CD carry the standards, so quality does not depend on who happens to be watching.

  3. People grow with real ownership

    I set goals, review performance and mentor engineers. I hand people a delivery early enough for it to stretch them. I have run more than 25 technical interviews and helped onboard new engineers.

  4. Plan for the handoffs

    Much of the risk in a delivery sits between teams: product, QA and DevOps. I coordinate those handoffs deliberately, from sprint planning through release readiness.
